# Story working notes

These notes preserve the author's initial premise. They are not a finished script or a replacement for the author's story. Unresolved details remain open.

## Premise

Gods secretly shape civilizations and manipulate them into wars. They implanted greed in humanity to encourage the conflict through which they harvest the nourishment of life and death.

After thousands of years, Earth's output no longer satisfies them. The planet enters a harvesting phase: the termination of all life when its civilization fails to produce enough.

A god living among humanity has found love. His refusal to accept Earth's destruction makes him a fallen god. He comes to understand how deeply humans attach themselves to their lives and the little things within them. When Earth's destruction approaches, he initially sees two choices: take the woman he loves away, or let her die.

He finds a third option: sacrificing himself to delay Earth's destruction. The other gods respond out of spite. They impose an ordeal meant to corrupt what he saved, creating portals and forcing participating civilizations to harvest renewable amounts of life energy from one another to meet the gods' demands. The gods handle collection; the civilizations bear the cost. Overharvesting can drain a world's living foundations.

## Hidden lore: not initial player-facing exposition

- The earlier exile/abyss origin has been superseded by his fall through defiance.
- Before his sacrifice, he leaves a hidden blessing in his lover that passes through her descendants. The working direction is that Earth cannot be located as a new inbound portal destination, although its own expeditions can return. The gods neglect the world while collections continue. Details of the blessing's eventual discovery remain open.
- The player is a lost child of a god, raised among humans. They gradually discover their difference and what their kind does.
- The eventual choices under consideration are to end the cycle, preserve it and join the gods, or continue harvesting other planets to keep Earth alive.

## Causal details still to define

These are design questions, not decisions:

- What does harvesting a planet involve, and what happens to its inhabitants?
- How does harvesting another world protect Earth from its own termination?
- What are the exact limits of the inherited blessing, and what action exposes it to the gods?

The first hub test does not need to answer or reveal all of these. It should remain compatible with the author's later answers.
