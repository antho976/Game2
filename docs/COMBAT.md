# Directional sword combat

The sparring yard is east of the school at (19, -19). There is no transition into a fight. The swordsman watches whoever comes near and squares up the moment the player is close in front of him (about five metres) or right beside him; the bout is on where everyone stands, with no teleport, salute or banner. A swing can be thrown anywhere in the village: LMB or Q outside a bout swings the drawn sword (drawing it first if a greatsword is equipped), and if the swordsman is within six metres that swing is the first blow of the bout. In the yard the sword goes back on the shoulder a few seconds after a bout with nobody to fight; X draws and sheathes it by hand anywhere. F near him also challenges him. There is no yielding: once blades are out the bout ends only when one fighter falls, or the player leaves the yard. After a bout ends he lets the player walk off for six seconds before he squares up again. F6 from free hub movement teleports to the yard and begins practice.

The combat skills screen (K) also picks the opponent the yard fields: a Recruit, the Swordsman or a Master. The three differ in health and damage, but above all in what a perfect parry does to them (see Openings, stagger and executions). No sword purchase is required: an unequipped player borrows the Warden greatsword for the bout. Owned equipment is used if equipped. Loan equipment never enters the inventory.

The design is a read-and-answer duel in four lanes, read from the player's side: High, Right, Low, Left. The same lane index is a block for the defender and a cut for the attacker, so a Right guard meets a Right cut. Blade choreography is authored in that screen space and mirrored onto each rig, so a Right guard sits on the player's right for both fighters, in third person and in first person.

## Blade and hands

The greatsword's own axes carry the choreography: +y runs up the blade and +x is the edge, and every key is written as a hilt point, a tip direction and an edge direction. Cuts keep the edge leading through the arc, and each cover turns an edge, never the flat, toward the cut it answers. The four covers have four silhouettes. High is a roof: the blade held horizontal above the head, point to the forehand side so the arms stay uncrossed. Right and Left are tall covers on that side, the blade climbing outward from the chest with the edge turned out. Low is a hanging point, the blade dropped in front of the belly and thighs. Each cover has its own stance (ducked under the roof, turned into a side cover, folded over the hanging point), and a held guard breathes rather than freezing.

Both hands are solved onto the grip as fists. The lead hand sits against the cross and the trailing hand at the pommel; the fingers run on from the forearm across the grip and the thumb points up the blade, so the wrist sits a fist short of the grip line instead of on it. The lead hand is the one whose shoulder the forehand cut is thrown from: the +x side of the authored space. The rig's bones named R hang on the character's own left, so the player's rig and the first-person arms lead with Hand.L while the partner, who sees the same lanes mirrored, leads with Hand.R. Every hilt is placed within the villager rig's half-metre reach for both hands, and the suite measures that.

Swings flow from wherever the blade already is. The bout records the pose it last showed for each fighter, and a cut, a buffered cut, a feint or a cut thrown out of a guard draws its chamber from that pose rather than from a fixed start; the execution's deep wind likewise starts from the strike that found the mark. A blocked cut does not carry through the target: the edge stops short, springs back off the guard and checks the chest. On the defender's side a block drives the guard along the cut and back before it springs into place, and a perfect parry beats the blade out against the cut with the chest driving in behind it. A hit knocks the blade along the lane the blow came from. The partner's guard snaps into each new lane with the same overshoot as the player's, so his cover can be read on his blade as well as in the reticle. In first person the sword hangs lower and farther from the eye, and a chamber drawn back behind the head stays a forearm ahead of the lens so the hilt never passes through the view.

## Controls

WASD moves. Settings → Camera selects aim lock or hard lock. Aim lock leaves the view free and follows the visible enemy under the pointer in overhead/far views or near the screen centre in first/third person. Hard lock keeps the same opponent and steers the first/third-person view toward it. T releases the lock or reacquires the pointed enemy. X draws or sheathes the equipped sword; a toggle during an attack waits until recovery finishes. Arrows select the lane. The mouse does too: in the overhead views the lane is simply where the cursor sits around the opponent, and in first and third person the mouse is captured during a bout and a short flick picks it. A flick is measured against its own direction, so reversing from one side to the other reads at once. A swing already in the air only feints on a deliberate, longer flick, so stray mouse motion never restarts a cut. LMB is a light cut, Q or Shift+LMB a heavy one, RMB the guard, Space a quickstep. K opens combat skills outside a bout; K or Escape closes it.

Two things can happen inside a swing. Changing lane in the first half of a windup is a feint: the cut restarts from its chamber in the new lane for 6 stamina, once per swing. Pressing guard in the first half of a windup pulls the blow for 4 stamina and leaves an ordinary guard up, never a perfect one. An attack pressed in the last 0.22 seconds of a recovery is buffered and starts the moment the fighter is free; a guard that is still held after a recovery rises again as an ordinary guard.

## Reading the duel

Four small triangles with red outlines sit over the opponent's chest, one per lane. The player's lane fills gold, brighter while guarding and white while a perfect window is open. His guard is a small blue triangle inside the lane it covers; a white pulsing one is sharp, meaning he is set to parry that lane, so feint or change lane. The incoming cut fills its triangle red and pushes it outward as the blade comes in, jumping lanes with a FEINT flash when he feints; a heavy cut wears a second outline. Timing is read off his blade, not off a ring. The instant the cut commits, a pale spark bursts on the blade: a guard raised now will meet it. Through the final 0.20 seconds a sharp red spark burns on the blade, the triangle turns red and a short tick sounds: a guard raised now is a perfect one. The spark follows the weapon at a fixed screen size in every camera, stops at impact or interruption, and planned feints suppress it until the real cut. A gold ring in the centre is the counter window and marks the opposite lane. While his guard is frozen, OPEN shows above the reticle, the stuck lane holds its blue triangle, the other three outlines pulse gold and a blue ring drains with the time left. While he is exhausted, a white circle with a red outline pulses on the weak-spot triangle, EXHAUSTED blinks above, an orange ring drains with the window and a glowing mark sits on his body where the blow must go. A hit taken flashes the lane it came from together with the matching screen edge. The enemy's health and exhaustion sit in a separate nameplate above its head. The compact player dock contains health and stamina with a thin exhaustion strip. Skill tiles, XP text, rings and bottom shortcut text have been removed. Combat rules, forms and conditional skills still apply.

## Rules

Matching lanes block. A newly raised matching guard within 0.20 seconds of impact is perfect and costs no stamina. Held blocks cost 18 stamina against light attacks and 28 against heavies; a blocked heavy still chips 12% of its damage through. Changing lanes does not reset timing; rapid taps have a 0.32-second rearm lock. Perfect blocks inflict 38 attacker exhaustion, throw the attacker into a 1.05-second recovery, and open a 1.05-second counter opportunity. Any attack begun inside the counter window is a riposte with a 22% shorter windup; the Turning edge skill makes an opposite-lane riposte deal 65% more damage. An attack spends the one-strike counter opportunity even if it misses.

Two swings committed to the same lane clash: neither lands, both fighters are thrown into a 0.55-second recovery, both lose 8 stamina and both chains reset. A hit interrupts a swing that is less than 40% through its windup; a committed light swing carries on through a light hit, so late trades happen; a heavy hit interrupts anything. A missed swing adds 0.14 seconds of recovery and the swordsman punishes it.

Two forms are known from the start. Crossing cut is Left, Right, High; its finisher forces its way through a held guard for 70% damage and 20 exhaustion instead of being blocked. Serpent's coil is Low, High, Low; its finisher deals 50% more and adds 30 exhaustion to a blocking guard. Cuts must connect (hit or be blocked) within 1.8 seconds of each other, and taking a hit resets the chain.

Quickstep lasts 0.28 seconds, with invulnerability bounded to its middle 0.20 seconds. It uses normal character collision and costs 24 stamina, reduced to 19 by Economy of motion. Weapon stamina, speed, damage and upgrades apply to attacks. Armor protection reduces incoming damage. Research health, stamina and recovery bonuses remain additive with leveling. Below 20% stamina a fighter is winded: windups are 15% slower and footwork 15% slower.

A guard without sufficient stamina breaks. Attacks resolve once at the strike phase and only within 2.65 metres. This is a timing-and-range duel, not per-triangle sword collision.

## Openings, stagger and executions

A perfect parry knocks his blade wide and it stays there: his guard is frozen in the parried lane and he can neither move it nor attack. Striking that lane still meets steel and is blocked, adding 14 to his stagger bar; striking any other lane lands for 25% more damage and ends the opening. How long he stays open depends on the kind of opponent, not on level: a Recruit for 2.4 seconds, the Swordsman for 1.3, and a Master never opens at all. Every perfect parry also fills his stagger bar, by 30 against a Recruit, 38 against the Swordsman and 48 against a Master, so the Master is beaten through the bar rather than through openings. Sustained pressure against a held guard fills it too: 12 per light and 26 per heavy blocked, more with the Breaking stroke skill and the forms. The stagger bar is the thin strip under his health on his nameplate.

When the stagger bar reaches 100 he is exhausted for three seconds: he cannot guard, every cut lands harder (light cuts for 50% more, heavies for 75% more), and a mark shows on his body in one of the four lanes. A heavy cut into that lane is the execution. The bout hands itself to a short in-game cinematic: the camera drops to a low side shot while the player coils into a full-body turning cut, swings over the shoulder for the blow, and the edge lands with a long freeze, a white flash and the biggest number in the game, three times a heavy cut. Then everyone is let go: the mark is gone, his stagger bar is empty and he is back on guard. Missing the window, or landing heavies elsewhere, gets only the ordinary exhausted bonus, and when the three seconds are up he gathers himself and the mark is gone. The player can be exhausted the same way, in which case the enemy's heavy is a finishing blow at double damage. Mine Wardens follow the Swordsman's rules.

## The sparring partner

He keeps a preferred distance of about two metres, circles when he has it, closes when he does not, and gives ground to breathe when his stamina falls under 25. He prefers to attack lanes the player is not guarding, occasionally throws a double (a second quick cut from another lane before the guard resets), and punishes a missed swing immediately. When the player begins a swing he reads the shown lane after a short delay and usually answers it; a feint halves that chance and delays his second read. From level 2 he feints himself, from level 3 he sometimes sets a sharp guard that parries the shown lane, and each level shortens his reactions and windups a little. His health grows by 12 per player level and his damage by 1.2. All of this is tuned for readability first: every attack is telegraphed on the reticle and above his head. Between bouts he stands his ground, turns to watch the player approach, and draws the moment he has them in front of him.

## Animation

Both fighters are animated procedurally on top of the shared idle and walk clips. The blade is authored as a set of keys in the player's screen space, each a hilt point, a direction the tip points in and a direction the edge faces, and mirrored onto each rig; a two-bone solver keeps both hands on the grip through every key. Guards are the covers of two-handed fencing: a crown over the head for High, the blade angled up to either side for Right and Left, a hanging point for Low. Out of guard the point hovers at the opponent's face and drifts a little toward the chosen lane. A cut is chambered behind the shoulder (an overhead from above it, diagonals from beside it, the rising cut low behind the hip), follows an arc through a mid point with the edge leading, accelerates so that it is at full speed at contact, and leaves contact at that speed into the follow-through before returning to guard; nothing slows down before the hit. A heavy is chambered farther back and trembles at the top before it drops. A whiff overreaches and drags the shoulders after it; a parried blade is flung wide and takes its time coming back; a frozen guard is held out where it was met, the wrists straining against it. The body runs the same way through a posture layer applied before the arm solver: hips lead the shoulders into a coil, the chest drives through the cut with the knees bending as the hips sink and the pelvis sliding over the front foot, the head stays on the opponent, a hit flinches away from its lane and doubles into a reel when staggered, an open fighter is thrown onto his back foot, and an exhausted one is bent over his dropped point, heaving.

## Feedback

The feedback layer is tuned for weight. Impacts freeze the frame by their heft: 40 milliseconds for a light block, 70 for a light hit, 120 for a parry, 140 for a heavy hit or a clash and 200 for finishing and critical blows, with the attacker's blade snapped to full extension before the freeze so the frozen frame shows the cut. The camera shakes and also recoils away from the lane that landed, rolling on side cuts and shoving forward with the field of view punching wider when the player's own blow connects. Landed cuts throw dark blood away from the blade and leave marks on the yard that fade over about ten seconds. A heavy hit, a critical or a finishing blow staggers: the victim reels for 0.46 seconds instead of 0.26, is knocked back twice as far and shudders through it, and both fighters now take that knockback through their movement. Committed swings drive the body forward over the last of the measure, heavy cuts plant the front foot with a stomp and a puff of dust, and the heavy blade trembles at the top of its chamber before it drops. Hits flash a light, throw sparks and land with a layered slam or thud under a wet burst; blocks clang, parries ring, guards rasp as they rise, and everything is synthesised at start-up because the repository has no sword recordings. Damage numbers land oversized and snap down, larger for heavies and largest for criticals and finishers; banners do the same. A freshly raised guard snaps past its mark, outward along its own cover, and settles. Blades leave a fading trail through the cut, longer and hotter for heavies and tinted red for the partner. A posture layer runs before the arm solver on both rigs: a forward-set ready stance, the chest coiling behind a chamber and driving through a cut (deeper for heavies), a flinch on hits that doubles into a reel when staggered, and a slump when exhausted. Being hit pulses a red vignette that brightens with the blow and throbs while under 30% health; criticals, finishers and perfect blocks add a brief white flash. Victory drops into slow motion and the beaten swordsman goes down on one knee until he is ready again; defeat slows too under a deep red. Banners call the salute, fight, perfect blocks, parries, feints, clashes, broken guards, completed forms, victory and defeat.

## Progression

A victory gives 60 XP and 25 gold, plus 20 XP for a flawless bout of at least eight seconds. The first level requires 100 XP, increasing by 40 each level. Every level grants one skill point, six health, four stamina, four percent base weapon damage and one protection. Equipment's existing level gates use this same level. Two three-node combat branches mix passive benefits and conditional moves. Purchases, XP and skill learning use the existing transactional save path. Failed saves roll back rewards or skill purchases. Defeat and leaving the arena do not destroy equipment; the partner resets after three seconds.

## Mine encounters

`expedition.gd` reuses this controller in encounter mode for eight Tithe Wardens. This swaps the trainer rig and sword for the authored Warden and mining blade, omits the training floor and player teleport, carries health, and emits completion or retreat signals. Encounter strikes require clear scenery line of sight. Navigation around the mine's machinery is supplied by the expedition controller. The original village sparring behavior and reward rules remain intact. Expedition rewards are held until the player returns through the gate; see `FIRST_PORTAL.md` for save, defeat and banking behavior.
