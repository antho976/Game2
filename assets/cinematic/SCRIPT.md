# Before the First Gate

We called them gods.

Beneath their gaze, we raised our kingdoms, and buried our fathers.
We knew not who had sown the hunger in our hearts.
Only that whatever we took, we hungered still.

And as we warred, they fed.

Yet there came an age when even our ruin could no longer sustain them.
They turned their eyes upon the living.
All that remained was to be gathered.

But one among them refused.

He had dwelt beneath our roofs.
There was one who waited for his return.
Through her, he came to know why we clung so fiercely to our days.

For her sake, he stood against his kind.
For ours, he gave his life.

His sacrifice stayed their hand.
They would not forgive him for it.

In their wrath, the gods opened the gates,
and laid their decree upon those he had spared.

“Thy world shall endure, so long as another bears its hunger.
What we are owed, thou shalt take from beyond these gates.
Spare the root, that thou mayest reap again.
Shouldst thou return empty-handed...
we shall gather what remains of thee.”

Thus were we spared.

We, who had prayed for mercy,
went forth to take what others could not bear to lose.

And behind us, our children lived.
